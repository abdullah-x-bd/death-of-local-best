# Literature Boundary: What Is Already Known and What Must Be New

## Closest prior theory

Tianxi Wang and Greg C. Wright (2020), Journal of Economic Theory:
"Increasing returns to scale within limits: A model of ICT and its effect on the income distribution and occupation choice."

This is the most important novelty constraint.

Their model already establishes that ICT can increase workers' scale of operation, intensify competition, increase within-occupation inequality, push lower-ability workers out, and reduce earnings for the next layer of workers.

Therefore this project must not claim novelty for the generic statement:
"the internet lets local workers compete with distant workers."

## Adjacent causal evidence

### Broadband rollout

Akerman, Gaarder and Mogstad (QJE 2015)
Uses a Norwegian public broadband rollout program to obtain plausibly exogenous variation in firm broadband adoption and finds heterogeneous labor-market effects by skill/task.

Zuo (AEJ: Economic Policy 2021)
Uses Comcast Internet Essentials with geographic eligibility, individual eligibility and timing in a triple-difference design.

BIP/BTOP evidence
Recent work reconstructs rejected broadband-program applicants as a comparison group, providing a feasible design template for U.S. program evaluation.

### Digital platform entry

Seamans and Zhu (Management Science 2014)
Uses temporal and geographic variation in Craigslist entry and newspaper dependence on classified advertising to estimate effects on local newspapers.

### Generative AI

Noy and Zhang (Science 2023)
Randomized ChatGPT access for professional writing tasks. Productivity rose and worker performance inequality fell.

Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (QJE 2025)
Staggered AI assistant rollout to 5,172 customer-support agents. Productivity gains were largest for less-skilled and less-experienced agents.

Yu, Immorlica, Jaffe, Liu and Monahan (2025 working paper)
Randomized Microsoft 365 Copilot access among freelancers on an online labor marketplace.

## Novelty target

The paper's contribution must be the integrated causal structure:

1. experimentally identify market integration × scalability as the mechanism that erodes middle-tier producer viability;
2. show in real local labor markets that broadband/digital entry reallocates tradable occupations according to pre-treatment local advantage;
3. distinguish loss of local occupational opportunity from national occupational decline;
4. show that AI is a second technological margin, changing competent-output scarcity rather than merely expanding human market reach;
5. connect both waves using one prespecified market-shelter framework.

## Contribution that would not be sufficient

Any of the following alone is too close to existing work:
- internet use correlates with occupational concentration
- ICT raises within-occupation inequality
- broadband changes employment
- AI raises worker productivity
- ChatGPT-exposed jobs decline after 2022

The high-end contribution requires the causal chain and cross-wave replication.
