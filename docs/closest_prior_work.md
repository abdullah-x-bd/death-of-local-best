# Closest prior work and novelty boundary

## Gottlieb, Hémous, Hicks, and Olsen

Their paper on top-income inequality contains a directly relevant methodological precedent.

They explicitly use Blinder's offshorability measure as an inverse proxy for whether an occupation serves a local market. Their model predicts stronger income-inequality spillovers for occupations whose services must be produced locally, and they test whether occupation-specific spillover estimates covary with offshorability and public interaction.

This is important for our project because it means the following idea is **not** novel:

> using occupation offshorability as a proxy for the degree to which an occupation serves a local market.

We should cite them directly wherever we use that interpretation.

## What remains distinct

Their treatment is local top-income inequality and their outcome is within-occupation income spillovers.

Our proposed treatment is technological reduction in geographic market frictions, initially broadband diffusion.

Our core outcomes are different:

- disappearance or persistence of occupations in local labor markets
- local employment shares
- geographic concentration
- migration/reallocation
- earnings
- occupational entry
- institutional versus independent work

Our mechanism is also temporally different:

1. geography protects locally scarce competence
2. digital connectivity reduces that protection
3. scalable platforms may concentrate demand
4. generative AI may then reduce scarcity of competent output itself

The strongest novelty claim therefore cannot be "some occupations serve local markets."

It must be something closer to:

> a causal test of whether digital connectivity erodes the local labor-market rents and occupational niches associated with geographically segmented competence, followed by a second-wave framework for machine-generated competence.

## Consequence for design

This prior work strengthens rather than weakens the case for Blinder-style offshorability as a moderator, because an independent Econometrica-level project already treats it as a proxy for local production.

But it raises the bar on our contribution.

We should therefore prioritize outcomes and designs that directly observe erosion of local niches over time rather than another cross-sectional correlation between offshorability and local-market behavior.
