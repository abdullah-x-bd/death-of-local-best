# Claim ladder

This document defines the maximum claim each empirical module is allowed to support.

## Claim 0: descriptive transformation

Statement:
Legacy local/intermediary employment structures changed sharply during digitization, while independent creative participation did not necessarily collapse.

Evidence:
national industry trends and creator/platform distributions.

Maximum language:
"documented", "coincided with", "is consistent with".

Not causal.

## Claim 1: broadband and remote-tradable occupations

Target statement:
Within the studied U.S. local labor markets and period, exogenous or plausibly exogenous broadband expansion caused a differential change in the local labor-market position of occupations whose services could be supplied from farther away.

Required before use:
- credible treatment variation
- no differential pre-trend of economically meaningful size
- predetermined tradability measure
- negative controls
- robustness across credible treatment definitions
- inference appropriate to treatment assignment
- no single occupation/region drives the result
- migration/reallocation separated from destruction

Maximum language if all requirements pass:
"Broadband expansion caused a differential change in ..."

If treatment remains endogenous:
"Broadband expansion was associated with a differential change in ..."

## Claim 2: erosion of local occupational niches

Target statement:
The causal broadband effect operates partly on the extensive geographic margin, reducing the probability that a tradable occupation remains represented in a local market or concentrating that occupation into fewer markets.

Additional requirement:
reliable occupation-presence measurement and evidence that changes are not artifacts of sample-cell noise.

Maximum language:
"Digital connectivity reduced local occupational presence / increased geographic concentration for the identified occupation set."

This still does not mean national occupational employment declined.

## Claim 3: worker destruction versus reallocation

Target statement:
The affected workers lose employment/earnings or exit the occupation rather than simply migrating or changing institution.

Additional requirement:
worker-level outcomes capable of distinguishing migration, occupation switching, self-employment, and earnings.

Possible conclusions:
- destruction
- geographic reallocation
- institutional reorganization
- increased self-employment
- mixed adjustment

The data decide which word is permitted.

## Claim 4: scalable-output/platform mechanism

Target statement:
A clean digital-platform shock to a local intermediary causes worker-level reallocation or occupational loss beyond the intermediary itself.

Leading candidate:
Craigslist × pre-treatment classified-ad exposure.

This is a separate causal module from broadband/offshorability.

## Claim 5: competence-rent interpretation

Target statement:
The combined causal evidence is consistent with the interpretation that geography previously created economic rents for locally scarce competence and that digital integration eroded part of those rents.

This is a structural interpretation, not a directly observed variable.

To strengthen it, the empirical results should show heterogeneity exactly where the model predicts and fail where it predicts no effect.

## Claim 6: generative AI second wave

Current maximum claim:
Experimental and credible workplace evidence shows that generative AI can increase productivity and compress performance gaps in specific cognitive tasks. This provides a mechanism through which skill scarcity rents could fall.

Not yet permitted:
"AI has causally eroded competence rents across the economy."

That stronger claim requires its own identified labor-market design.

## Final epistemic rule

The paper may establish several narrow causal facts very strongly.

No module is permitted to imply a universal law outside its treatment, population, time period, and identifying assumptions.

If the causal first-wave result fails, the paper must either reject the first-wave hypothesis or retreat to a descriptive/conceptual paper. It may not preserve the causal headline by changing treatments, outcomes, or samples after seeing the result.
