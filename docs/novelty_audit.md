# Novelty audit

Status: living document. Novelty claims are provisional until the systematic review is complete.

## 1. Conference Board: Manole and Weiss (2011)

### Question

Did ICT alter the geographic distribution of U.S. industry and occupational employment?

### Data

- QCEW industry-by-metropolitan-area employment for 1995, 2000, and 2005
- Census occupation-by-metropolitan-area employment for 1990 and 2000

### Occupational analysis

The study explicitly asks whether occupations viewed as more movable or offshorable changed geographic concentration more as broadband and the internet became prevalent.

It constructs a Geographic Concentration of Occupation index and relates changes to subjective assessments of whether occupations can be performed at a distance.

### Authors' own limitation

The study states that its occupational analysis is only suggestive because many economic factors changed between 1990 and 2000 and because the mobility indexes are subjective.

### Consequence for our novelty

We cannot claim to be the first to ask whether ICT changes the geographic distribution of more movable/offshorable occupations.

Potential contribution:

- exploit within-period local broadband diffusion rather than two national Census snapshots
- use predetermined occupation-level exposure
- add high-dimensional fixed effects or a quasi-experimental design
- focus on local occupational presence and the extensive margin
- distinguish migration, occupational exit, and geographic reallocation
- connect the first digital wave to a separate AI competence-scarcity mechanism

Source:
The Conference Board, The Linked World, chapter "The Impact of ICT on the Geographic Distribution of Employment," 2011.

## 2. Bergeaud, Malgouyres, Mazet-Sonilhac, and Signorelli (2025)

### Question

Does staggered broadband diffusion change domestic outsourcing and firm occupational organization?

### Setting

France during broadband diffusion in the 2000s.

### Main findings

Connected firms increase outsourcing and reduce occupational diversity in-house. Employment in non-core occupations becomes increasingly concentrated in firms specializing in subcontracting services. High-skill and low-skill outsourced workers have different wage consequences.

### Overlap

Very close to our institutional-reorganization mechanism.

It shows that broadband can change how occupations are bundled across firms and can concentrate employment in specialist firms.

### Distinction

Their concentration object is across firms and outsourcing relationships, not geographic disappearance of local occupations or erosion of geographic scarcity rents.

### Consequence

Our paper must cite this prominently and should treat institutional reorganization as an alternative/complementary pathway rather than presenting it as a new conjecture.

## 3. Djourelova, Durante, and Martin (2025)

### Question

What happens to local newspapers when Craigslist enters local classified-ad markets?

### Design

Staggered Craigslist introduction across U.S. counties between 1995 and 2009, interacted with newspapers' ex ante reliance on classified advertising.

### Findings

More exposed newspapers cut newsroom and management staff, including political editors, and reduce political coverage.

### Overlap

This is strong causal evidence that a digital platform can destroy resources inside a local intermediary.

### Distinction

The paper studies newspaper organizations and political-news production, not whether displaced journalistic competence disappears, migrates, enters self-employment, or survives outside newspapers.

### Potential extension for Module B

Use Craigslist as a clean digital-platform shock and study occupation-level local labor-market outcomes around exposed newspaper markets, if the labor microdata and treatment geography permit.

This would directly connect institutional destruction to worker reallocation.

Replication files are publicly linked from Gregory Martin's Stanford page.

## 5. Atasoy (2013) and Kolko (2012)

Both exploit early U.S. broadband diffusion to study local aggregate economic/labor outcomes.

Overlap: broadband as local treatment.

Distinction: neither makes our occupation-by-tradability/local-niche outcome the central estimand.

## 6. Current defensible novelty target

The most defensible candidate contribution is:

> causally estimating whether digital connectivity erodes the geographic persistence and local labor-market position of occupations whose services can be supplied across distance, with explicit decomposition into disappearance, migration, occupational exit, and institutional reorganization.

A second, conceptually separate contribution is:

> distinguishing remote labor tradability from scalable digital output, then connecting both first-wave mechanisms to the emerging second-wave reduction in the scarcity of competent cognitive output from generative AI.

## 7. Claims explicitly prohibited for now

Do not write:

- "No previous research has studied this."
- "This is the first paper to study ICT and occupational geography."
- "Offshorability as a measure of localness is novel."
- "Broadband caused the national collapse of creative work."
- "AI has already caused economy-wide destruction of competence rents."

These statements are unsupported or contradicted by prior work.
