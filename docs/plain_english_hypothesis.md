# Plain-English hypothesis and falsification rule

## The big idea

People used to earn a living partly because they were good relative to the people nearby.

Technology can remove that protection.

The internet can make distant humans compete with local humans.

AI can make competent output itself much cheaper and more abundant.

## Main causal hypothesis for Wave 1

When fast internet reaches a place, occupations whose work can be supplied from far away should lose more of their local protection than occupations that still require physical local presence.

In plain English:

> If the internet really destroys geographic scarcity, then after broadband expands, locally protected but digitally tradable occupations should shrink, disappear, relocate, or lose earnings more than otherwise similar local-only occupations in the same place and period.

## What would count as support

Support requires all of the following:

1. The affected occupations do not already show the same differential trend before treatment.
2. The effect appears after the broadband shock, not before it.
3. The pattern is stronger for occupations that are genuinely tradable at distance.
4. The same pattern does not appear in low-tradability placebo occupations.
5. The result survives reasonable alternative definitions of geography, treatment, occupation mapping, and inference.
6. The result is not driven by one city, one state, one occupation, the Great Recession, or COVID.
7. Migration and industry switching are measured separately so geographic reallocation is not mislabeled as job destruction.

## What would count as rejection

The Wave 1 hypothesis is rejected or substantially weakened if:

- high-tradability occupations were already declining before broadband
- low-tradability occupations change just as much
- the result disappears under credible treatment definitions
- the result is caused by a known reporting break
- the effect is entirely explained by one occupation or one region
- migration explains the apparent local disappearance with no evidence of a labor-market loss
- alternative credible tradability measures reverse the result

## Module B hypothesis

A second first-wave mechanism concerns scalable output rather than offshorable labor.

Plain English:

> When a digital platform directly attacks the local business model that pays for a local skill, the institution should shrink first. We then test whether the workers themselves disappear from the occupation, move elsewhere, become independent, or enter other industries.

Craigslist and local newspapers are the leading candidate setting.

## Wave 2 hypothesis

The AI hypothesis is different.

Plain English:

> If AI makes competent cognitive output much easier to produce, then the market value of being merely competent should fall most in work where AI can perform or strongly assist the core tasks.

At present, this will be treated as a mechanism-and-early-evidence section unless we find a genuinely clean quasi-experiment.

## Epistemic rule

No study in this project is allowed to answer a question broader than its design identifies.

The paper will distinguish:

- descriptive facts
- causal estimates
- mechanism evidence
- hypotheses

A result is causal only if its identification assumptions are stated and survive the prespecified falsification tests.

The goal is not to make every result support the theory.

The goal is to design tests that can force us to reject it.
